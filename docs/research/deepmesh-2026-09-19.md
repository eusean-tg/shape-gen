# DeepMesh comparison: simple peasant and Makima

Date: 2026-09-19 MYT. Isolated local experiment on RTX 3050 8 GiB.
The intended use is simple low-poly art. The teal peasant is the primary test;
Makima is a secondary stress test. No API defaults or accepted character files
are replaced by this experiment.

## Conclusion

**Keep the existing BPT-based low-poly workflow and pause further model
experiments for now.** DeepMesh ran successfully on both inputs, but neither
candidate is a compelling replacement. The peasant keeps a reasonable silhouette
and has more defined sleeve/hair rims, yet introduces visible holes in the arms
and substantially worse connectivity at over three times the original BPT
triangle count. Makima loses the continuity of its hair despite a larger mesh.

This is one seed on two inputs, not a claim that DeepMesh can never perform
better. No seed sweep, texture work or salvage/retopology project was started.
The installed environment, weights, point clouds, completed tokens and review
artifacts are retained for a future revisit. The accepted textured/animated
peasant and API defaults remain unchanged.

| Result | Triangles | Boundary edges | Edges shared by >2 faces | Elapsed |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Teal peasant, original BPT | 1,458 | 6 | 2 | 63.61 s |
| Teal peasant, DeepMesh | 4,606 | 158 | 189 | 499.67 s |
| Makima, original BPT | 2,409 | 15 | 0 | 112.94 s |
| Makima, DeepMesh | 5,845 | 19 | 0 | 526.49 s |

These are the raw comparison meshes after their ordinary export cleanup, not
the peasant's later repaired production topology. More triangles, consistent
normals, or fewer overconnected edges alone do not establish better geometry.

## Teal peasant result

EOS arrived after 16,232 tokens; generation took 494.30 seconds. The output has
2,228 vertices and 4,606 triangles. The broad clothing silhouette is recognisable,
and some rims around the sleeves and hair are more pronounced. Side renders
show large dark openings in the forearms; knee-breeches have a pronounced ring
at the knees. The added surface complexity does not yield a cleaner character
for the intended simple style.

The topology check finds 158 boundary edges and 189 overconnected edges, despite
consistent winding. Trimesh reports 132 face-adjacency groups, with 4,218 faces
in the largest group. Non-manifold edges can split this adjacency graph, so this
must not be interpreted as exactly 132 spatially detached objects. It does
establish a much less straightforward repair than the original BPT candidate.

The original BPT run took 63.61 seconds overall. DeepMesh took roughly eight
minutes here and did not justify replacing the existing repaired, rigged result.

Peak PyTorch allocation was 3,739.7 MiB for both completed DeepMesh trials;
reserved memory peaked at 3,804.0 MiB. These figures exclude desktop use, driver
contexts and some native allocations. Both runs fit the actual 8 GiB card.

## Makima result

DeepMesh completed with EOS after 16,822 generated tokens: 5,845 triangles,
2,943 vertices, 526.49 seconds total (521.12 seconds generation). Peak PyTorch
allocation was 3,739.7 MiB, excluding desktop/context/native overhead.

The mesh has consistent winding, 19 boundary edges, no edges with more than two
incident faces, and ten connected components. Those counts do not imply a good
reconstruction: side renders show the rear braid broken into detached rounded
pieces, front hair sections are mostly lost, and separate pieces appear around
the shoulders. The face looks mask-like and the rear-knee folds remain angular.
This is not an improvement over the original 2,409-triangle BPT for this asset,
despite DeepMesh spending substantially more geometry and generation time.

Disconnected components have 3,594, 316, 316, 296, 296, 244, 244, 210, 210 and
119 faces. They are substantial pieces, not merely a few disposable triangles.
Their measurements are saved in `makima/components.json`.

## Inputs and method

Both DeepMesh and the existing BPT comparison use the same Hunyuan dense source
for each character. This preserves the multiview information already in the source
geometry: four views for Makima; front, left and back for the teal peasant. No image generation or shape regeneration is involved.

| Character | Dense source | Existing BPT |
|---|---|---|
| Teal peasant | `assets/teal-peasant/dense/01-dense.glb`, 29,822 triangles | 1,458 triangles; raw BPT before the accepted character's subsequent repairs |
| Makima | `assets/makima-geometry-review/dense/result/01-dense.glb`, 62,648 triangles | 2,409 triangles |

One DeepMesh candidate per character: seed 12345, temperature .5, 16,384 sampled
surface points and normals. Sampling follows the official 50k-area-sample
recipe and ZXY coordinate convention, with explicitly seeded RNGs and saved
conditioning arrays. The source is centered and normalized to a longest extent
of 1.9, following its normalization helper. A source-coordinate export and an
upstream-coordinate diagnostic are retained.

The learned decoder output is already in the source XYZ convention even though
the point conditioner consumes ZXY. This was checked against source alignment
over discrete axis/sign mappings. The initial assumed inverse permutation was
corrected by re-exporting the unchanged completed tokens; no new generation or
surface deformation was used. The final exporter restores source scale and
center only. The official script's reverse-axis output is retained separately.

The official model configuration is `Diff_LLaMA_551M`. The full loaded checkpoint
contains 908,149,121 parameters including the point encoder and associated
modules. We use strict weight loading and verify the publisher's checkpoint
SHA256. This avoids assuming the advertised rounded generator size describes
the entire resident pipeline.

The official sampler's Gumbel-noise temperature formula and 9,000-token sliding
window are retained. The experiment uses batch one, a 30k-token maximum and a
900-second generation-time bound. Those bounds are runtime controls, not target
triangle counts. Completed output requires an EOS token; incomplete sequences
are retained as diagnostic tokens rather than exported as successful meshes.

## Memory adaptations

The independent `.venv-deepmesh` uses Torch 2.6/cu124 and FlashAttention 2.7.4.
Weights remain FP32 with BF16 autocast as in upstream sampling. The point encoder
runs first and leaves GPU memory before the generator is loaded. There is no
weight quantization, distributed launch, or simultaneous batch of four samples.

Two legacy CUDA-extension wrappers are replaced in an isolated copy of `lit_gpt`:
rotary embedding and RMSNorm call FlashAttention's corresponding Triton kernels.
The operations and checkpoint tensor names are preserved; bit-identical output
to the legacy kernels is not claimed. Source and wrapper hashes are saved in
`assets/makima-deepmesh/code-manifest.json`. The official checkout is unchanged.

## Review criteria

Compare full silhouettes, head/hair shapes, sleeves, knees, hands and feet in
matching neutral-clay renders. A simple faceless character does not need anime
facial detail. More output triangles do not automatically establish a better
low-poly result; counts are shown explicitly. Surface gaps, normal consistency,
component counts and non-manifold edges are measured separately from appearance.
Neither successful generation nor an attractive static render establishes
usable skinning or clean animation deformation.

No texture or rig work is included. The existing peasant animation remains the
accepted asset. Generated output receives vertex merging, duplicate/degenerate
face removal, removal of unused vertices and normal correction only. No manual
repair, smoothing, surface wrapping or hole filling is applied to the raw
comparison result.

## Reproduction and sources

The rotary and RMSNorm adapters matched direct FP32-accumulation reference math
after BF16 rounding on the test tensors (maximum absolute difference zero).
The serializer roundtrip stayed within one quantization-cell diagonal. These
are local operation checks, not a bit-identical full-model comparison.
`adapter-validation.json` records the results. Source-coordinate alignment and
finite geometry are checked in `coordinate-validation.json`.

Browser review loaded all six model entries and fetched all 36 gallery images
without missing resources or page errors; see `preview-validation.json`.
